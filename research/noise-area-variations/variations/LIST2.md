# The variation list, second list

None of V1 to V8 passed. The design allows one second list, fixed from the measuring pass and from the first list's results on 2021 to 2023 only. This is it: four variations, fixed before any of them was run. Nothing in these files changes after this commit. There is no third list.

What the first list showed on 2021 to 2023 (harsh fills, in multiples of the risk per trade): one trade a day held to the close (V5) was best at +0.26, with the shortest losing run, 12 in a row. Buys carried the result; sells were about break-even. Every half-hour variation sat exactly at the seven-day limit, with no room. Every variation failed survival: the $2,000 limit was crossed in the real order of days.

So the second list starts from V5 and goes after the two bars that bind, survival and the seven-day rule. All four use the 9:30 AM band on the half hour, buys and sells, one trade a day, entries 9:31 AM to 3:30 PM, flat by 4:00 PM, a stop resting from the fill.

**W1, V5 at a smaller risk.** 0.2% ($100 a trade), which is 20 losers of room in place of 16. Below $100 the measuring pass says too many breakouts are skipped to keep the seven-day rule.

**W2, W1 with a trailing stop.** The stop follows the best price at three times the risk, as in V7. On 2021 to 2023 V7 won 37.5% of the time against 32.2% for V2 on the same entries; more winners means shorter losing runs.

**W3, a wider stop.** Three quarters of the band width, at 0.3% ($150) so one contract still fits. A wider stop is overshot by less on harsh fills and wins more often; three quarters is the widest that the measuring pass suggests can still average a winner of twice the risk. This exact stop was not measured.

**W4, a narrower band.** The band at 0.8 of its normal width, otherwise W1. A narrower band is crossed more often, which gives room on the seven-day rule, and its stop is smaller, so fewer breakouts are skipped. This band width was not measured.

Not tried, and why: buys only (the measuring pass shows a 14-day stretch without one); the evening and overnight hours (flat, losing, or a lottery that cannot keep the seven-day rule); a risk under $100 (too many skips for the seven-day rule).

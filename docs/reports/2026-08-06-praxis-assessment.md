## 1. What PRAXIS is

PRAXIS is an attempt to build an automated futures-trading system that takes a trade signal generated by a charting platform and turns it, without a human in the loop, into a real bracket order at a broker.

The intended chain runs end to end like this. A strategy running on **TradingView** (a charting and alerting service) fires an alert. That alert hits a webhook served by **n8n** (a self-hosted workflow automation tool, running in Docker on Amear's Mac). n8n validates the alert and writes it as a JSON file into a shared folder. On the other side of that folder sits **NinjaTrader 8** ("NT8" — the desktop trading platform, running in a Windows virtual machine under Parallels), where a custom program written in **NinjaScript** (NinjaTrader's C# dialect) watches the folder with a FileSystemWatcher, picks up each new file, and submits a **bracket order** — an entry order with a stop-loss and profit-target attached, where filling one cancels the other. NT8 routes that order through **Rithmic**, a futures order-routing provider, into an account at **MFFU**, a funded-account prop firm that puts up the trading capital in exchange for a share of profits and the right to impose rules. The instrument is **NQ**, the E-mini Nasdaq-100 futures contract.

Wrapped around that is a monitoring spine: git as the source of truth, a `STATUS.md` state file, Google Sheets, ClickUp, n8n, and Telegram alerts.

The build is organised into seven phases called **Blocks 0 through 6**. Block 0 was orchestration scaffolding — the webhook, the dashboard, the notification paths. Block 1 was signal delivery: getting a signal to actually reach NT8 and become an order on a simulated account. Block 2 is backtesting: proving the strategy has an edge before risking money. Block 3 involves the live-signal topology and a subsystem the repo calls **SHM**, which consumes statistical reference distributions derived from backtest results (the facts pack does not expand the acronym, and I have not verified what it stands for). Block 4 is paper trading against an acceptance gate. Blocks 5 and 6 are not characterised anywhere in the facts pack beyond "not started"; the only inference available is that Block 5 involves the funded account, since the MFFU compliance question blocks it.

Two more pieces of vocabulary are load-bearing.

A **bead** is a unit of tracked work in `bd`, the issue tracker this project uses instead of TODO lists. Beads have IDs like `Praxis_build-r8j`, priorities P0 through P3, and dependency links. When the assessment below names a three-character code, that is a bead.

The **agent stack** is a supervision system built inside this repo in the last two weeks of July 2026. It is a hierarchy of Claude sub-agents — a master, managers, auditors, workers — bound by shell hooks that intercept tool calls before they run. Its purpose is to prevent an AI agent from committing unaudited work, writing outside its lane, or grading its own output. Three of its concepts recur below. A **FROZEN criterion** is a named blocker written into `specs/SPEC_RUBRIC.md` that halts a whole Block until a specific artifact exists; there are four, F1 through F4. The **park rule** is a stop rule: when an audit fails twice on the same item, that work is "parked" and cannot proceed without an explicit ruling. An **audit token** is a single-use credential minted by a passing audit and bound to the exact staged file tree; `gate-commit.sh` refuses a commit that does not present a valid one, which is how the system enforces that a producer cannot also be its own grader.

## 2. Where it actually stands

The distinction that matters most here is between what Amear signed off on and what an agent asserted. They are not the same standard and the repo is much thinner on the first than the second.

**Block 0 is complete and trader-signed-off**, on 2026-07-09, recorded at commit `be920c1`. This is a real sign-off by a human.

**Block 1 is complete and trader-signed-off**, on 2026-07-15, against an independent evidence audit at `d405125` and recorded at `1d10921`. This is the strongest single fact about the project. It means a signal genuinely travelled the whole path — webhook, n8n, file drop, NinjaScript consumer, NT8 — and became a standing bracket order on a Sim101 simulated account, with Amear confirming the bracket visually in the NT8 GUI (`228b586`: "trader GUI-confirmed the 11:12:45 standing bracket, fill 29820, OCO stop 29741.25 / target 29901.25"). The plumbing works. That is not a small thing and it is not in dispute.

**Block 2 has been active since 2026-07-15 and is not complete.** It is where the project has sat for three weeks. The critical bead is `4uu` — import the NinjaScript strategy into NT8, compile it, and reconcile it against the Strategy Analyzer. `4uu` is **frozen behind five gates** and additionally requires Amear at the VM. The downstream chain that would actually establish an edge — `zi1` (walk-forward analysis: train on one window, test on the next, repeatedly, to see if the strategy survives out-of-sample), `ajj` (Monte Carlo simulation on the pooled out-of-sample trades), `xdr` (generate the reference distributions the Block-3 SHM consumer needs) — **has not started.**

**Blocks 3, 4, 5 and 6 have not started.** They are blocked by FROZEN criteria F1, F2 and F4, and by bead `r8j`.

And the sentence that should govern how you read everything else: **the nine circuit breakers are specified only. Zero implemented, zero tested.**

## 4. What is claimed but not verified

Apply the ladder honestly: **specified is not implemented, implemented is not tested, tested is not tested under failure.** Different parts of this project sit on different rungs, and the repo's own language does not always distinguish them.

**The nine circuit breakers sit below the bottom rung.** These are the numbered risk-control rules — the things that stop the system trading through a news event, or after a loss limit, or outside permitted hours. The facts pack names some of them by number: breaker #4 fires at 11:30, breaker #5 covers 15:00–18:00, breakers #2 and #3 interact with reverse-and-re-enter behaviour. The critical fact is what bead `r8j` exists to fix: **the Phase 3 Build Specification that defines them has never been imported into this repository.** So the breakers are specified — in a document that is not here. Nothing is implemented. Nothing is tested. Nothing has been tested under failure. This was recorded at the cross-block audit of 2026-07-25 and has not changed since.

That single fact cascades. FROZEN criterion **F1** requires a committed `docs/specs/*-live-signal-topology.md` naming what emits the live signal and showing all nine breakers upstream of order submission — you cannot write it without the spec. **F2** requires reconciling the Dev-B 16:00 ET session-flat rule against breakers #4 and #5, which the pack records as an outright collision (`ax4`) — you cannot resolve it without the breaker definitions. Beads `2nh`, `ax4`, `yra` and `qtk` are all blocked on `r8j`, which is why the pack flags it as ROOT CAUSE. **The system has no risk controls, and the document describing what they should be is not in the repository.**

**The strategy is implemented but never executed.** `719ab11` is source code. It has not been compiled inside NT8, has not been run through the Strategy Analyzer, and has not been reconciled against the paper. That is exactly what `4uu` is for, and `4uu` is frozen. There is no backtest. Therefore there is no evidence, of any kind, that this strategy has an edge. F3 makes this worse in a specific way: bead `zyd` requires pinning the intrabar fill and bar-resolution assumption *before* the backtest runs, because a backtest that silently assumes optimistic fills produces a number that is worse than no number.

**The agent stack was tested, then defeated.** Commit `b8008e4` claims "5 trip tests + depth-2 nesting proven." I did not verify those tests; that is a commit-message claim. But whatever they proved, the guards were subsequently broken by adversarial probing, and all three open P0 beads are those breaks: `s8g` (the master write guard exempts all subagents at line 8, and no hook registers on `.claude/agents/` at all), `hwk` (quoting the subcommand defeats the producer/grader separation entirely), `1ys` (the spawn guard's worker terminal-layer rule is bypassable by simply omitting `subagent_type`). This is the correct reading: the stack was tested against the failure modes its authors imagined, and it did not survive contact with failure modes they did not.

**Some claims rest on nothing.** Bead `cyd` records that the "four Bash hooks" claim rests on an ungraded, out-of-repo plugin hook. `sk8` records that the S9 registration-field positive control is undischarged — meaning a check was declared to work without ever being shown to fire. `cbf` records false agent-memory content that needs correcting. And an ISSUE_REGISTER row dated 2026-08-04 records that **two staged reports quote a sentence their target file does not contain.** My own prior audit memory on this repo names the same pattern from a different angle: ledger rows that claim in-place corrections to bead bodies which, checked against `bd show --json`, were never made.

**One PROVEN is agent-asserted.** Commit `27766ad` records the VM-agent delegation live-executor leg as PROVEN. The facts pack does not record trader confirmation of it. Treat it as agent-asserted, not signed off.

## 5. What keeps failing

These are patterns with multiple instances, not incidents.

**Guards fail open, and several fail silently.** Every defect in the supervision layer is a fail-open: `1ys` bypassed by omitting a field, `hwk` bypassed by quoting a string, `s8g` exempting everything at line 8, `kgp` where the quoted-region strip fails on multi-line commands. Worse are the silent ones. An ISSUE_REGISTER row dated 2026-07-26 records a "silent-disarm trip-wire": if a settings `agent` key points at an agent definition that is missing, the key resolves to nothing and **both master guards stop arming** — no error, no log, just an unprotected system that looks protected. The same week recorded that `Agent(<type>)` allowlists are **not enforced** on Claude Code 2.1.220, and that agent-frontmatter hooks are **silently ignored**. Two of the design's assumed enforcement mechanisms do not exist in the platform. A guard that fails silently is worse than no guard, because it manufactures confidence.

**The supervision system cannot operate under its own rules.** This is the pattern with the most instances, and they are deadlocks rather than bugs. The master cannot write its own persistent memory (2026-07-27). The auditor cannot write its own verdict to disk (2026-08-03). A denied commit burns the single-use audit token, so a rejection costs you the credential you need to retry (2026-08-05). `gate-commit.sh` reports the wrong cause when a token expires, so the operator debugs the wrong thing (2026-08-05). Parallel PreToolUse denials mask each other, so you cannot see which rule fired (2026-07-26). Bead `8sw` states it flatly: the absolute park rule has no sanctioned commit path — once work parks, there is no legal way to unpark it. Bead `y9c` has been parked since audit FAIL 2 and is still parked. There is a live instance in this very dispatch: I was asked to write this assessment to a file and my own instructions forbid writing report files. I am the eighth data point in this pattern.

**Context exhaustion is now a structural failure mode, and it has been measured.** The ledgers total **659 KB, roughly 160,000 tokens** — `DECISION_LOG.md` alone is 198,490 bytes across 163 rows, averaging 1,218 bytes per row. `docs/reports/` holds 92 files and 1.6 MB. Five consecutive auditor dispatches died on the same task family on 2026-08-06: at 47, 47, 49, 36 and 41 tool uses, one of them burning **186,424 tokens** before dying. (The brief that dispatched me says six; the pack, written the same day, tables five. The pack itself exists because of them.) Successful dispatches the same day finished in 11, 22 and 25 tool uses. The pack's diagnosis is precise and correct: **the variable is not brief size, it is how much ledger the brief causes the agent to read.** The append-only ledger is a defensible design that has crossed the threshold where the workers it governs can no longer read it.

**Claims outrun their evidence, specifically outside the diff.** Reports quoting sentences their targets do not contain. Rows claiming bead edits that never happened. A "four hooks" claim resting on an ungraded external file. A positive control declared but never fired. The consistent shape: in-diff claims are cheap to check and generally hold; **claims pointing outside the staged set are where this repo's failures live**, and they are the ones nobody checks.

**Work is produced and does not land.** Twenty-six of ninety-two reports exist only in the working tree — untracked, unpushed, invisible to anyone who clones this repo. Thirty-nine paths are dirty. Thirty-six beads open, **zero in progress**. Sessions 37, 38, 39, 40 and most of 41 closed with **zero commits**, an eleven-day stretch between `c1dec30` and `5067d32`. Meanwhile the project's own CLAUDE.md says work is not complete until `git push` succeeds.

## 6. What is holding the project back, ranked by leverage

**1. The MFFU compliance answer (F4 / bead `fx4`). This is the top blocker and it is not technical.** F4 requires written confirmation from MFFU that this automation pattern is permitted on a funded account. The facts pack names the owner: Amear. It also names the nature of the task: "Not a build task — an email." It blocks Block 4 and Block 5. Its leverage is asymmetric in a way nothing else on this list is: if the answer is *no*, then the backtest, the circuit breakers, the paper-trading gate and the live topology are all work toward a destination that does not accept the vehicle. This question has been open since 2026-07-25. Twelve days of build effort have been spent without knowing whether the target account permits the thing being built.

**2. Import the Phase 3 Build Specification (`r8j`).** This is a copy-and-reconcile task, not a research task. It unblocks `2nh`, `ax4`, `yra` and `qtk`, makes F1 writable and F2 resolvable, and it is the only path to the nine circuit breakers existing at all. The document exists; it is simply not here.

**3. Stop building supervision. This is a scope decision, reversible today.** All three P0s are supervision bugs. Every ISSUE_REGISTER row opened since 2026-07-26 is supervision. Seven of fourteen P1s are supervision. The supervision layer has become a self-sustaining source of work that generates its own audits, its own adversarial probes, its own reports and its own P0s, and none of it moves an order closer to a broker. Nothing external forces this to continue.

**4. Compact the ledgers.** At 659 KB they are now the rate limiter on every agent dispatch, at a demonstrated cost of up to 186,000 tokens per failed attempt. The facts pack is a workaround, and a good one; it is not a fix. Archive-and-index would restore the ability to dispatch work at all.

**5. Pin the fill-resolution assumption (F3 / `zyd`).** One decision, written down, unfreezes `4uu` — which is the gate in front of the entire Block-2 completion chain.

**6. Resolve the session-flat collision (F2 / `ax4`).** Genuinely blocked behind item 2; do not attempt before the spec is imported.

**7. Run the Block-2 chain (`zi1` → `ajj` → `xdr`).** This is the real work, correctly sequenced, and it cannot start until items 2 and 5 clear.

Items 1 and 3 are both Amear's, neither is technical, and neither takes a day. Everything below them is downstream of them.

## 7. Where the effort actually went

The data answers this without ambiguity, so I will state it without softening.

One hundred and fifteen commits: nine in May 2026, one hundred and five in July, one in August. The last commit that touches NinjaScript or strategy code is `719ab11`, **2026-07-16**. The last commit that adds trading-system functionality of any kind is `f23b648`, **2026-07-22** — the timezone conversion for the continuous NQ series. The last commit that advances the trading system in any form, including merely freezing and filing it, is dated **2026-07-25**.

Every commit after that — all six — is agent-supervision machinery. Every one of the 35 reports dated 2026-07-26 or later concerns guards, hooks, agent memory, park rules, audit gates or commit composition. **Zero reports dated after 2026-07-25 concern the trading system.** Every ISSUE_REGISTER row opened since 2026-07-26 concerns supervision. All three P0 beads are supervision.

So: **for the last fifteen days the project has written no trading-system code, and for the last twelve it has spent its entire effort building and then auditing the machinery that supervises the building.** That is the plain statement of what the commit and report timelines show, and it is not a matter of interpretation.

It gets sharper. The supervision machinery did not merely displace the build — it became the build's subject matter. The agent stack was installed on 2026-07-26. Within four days it had produced an adversarial actor hunt, a null attack, a worker trigger probe, an agent-key lab, an agent-key control, a park-rule escalation, a guard-evasion test, a live-vs-piped attack, negative closer tests, and three P0 vulnerabilities in itself. Thirty-five reports and roughly fifteen issue-register rows, all of them findings **about the supervisor**. The system built to make the work trustworthy became the only work, and then became the only thing found untrustworthy.

And it did not even hold the line it was built to hold. Sessions 37 through 41 — the period of most intense supervision-building — produced **zero commits over eleven days**, in a repository whose own standing rule is that work is not complete until it is pushed. Twenty-six reports sit untracked in the working tree. Thirty-six beads are open and none is in progress. The control system's measurable effect on throughput was to take it to zero.

The supervision work is not worthless. The three P0s are real vulnerabilities and finding `hwk` — that quoting a subcommand defeats producer/grader separation — was genuine adversarial work. The park rule and the audit token are sound ideas. But the honest accounting is this: **the defects the supervision layer has caught are, almost without exception, defects in the supervision layer.** In fifteen days it has not caught a single defect in the trading system, because the trading system has not changed in fifteen days. That is a closed loop, and closed loops feel productive from the inside because they generate artifacts at a steady rate. Ninety-two reports is an impressive number. Thirty-five of them, thirty-eight percent of the total, were produced in a thirteen-day window in which the actual product did not advance by one line.

The strongest evidence that this is the real problem rather than a rhetorical framing is item 1 in the previous section. The single highest-leverage open item in this project is an email to MFFU asking whether automated trading is permitted on a funded account. It has been open since 2026-07-25 — the exact day the project stopped building the trading system and started building the supervisor. If the answer to that email is *no*, then every commit since 2026-07-16 was spent on machinery to govern the construction of something that cannot be deployed.

**Send the email first.**
